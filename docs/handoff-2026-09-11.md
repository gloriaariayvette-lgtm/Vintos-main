## Part one — what the three current tasks are for

### 1. A want that changes his rate, not just his to-do list

**The problem she named.** He formed a want to analyse less and reach out less. Under
a to-do model that want has no artifact and no step that completes it, so it sat
unfulfillable while the analysing carried straight on. A want that describes how he
wants to *be* moved nothing in the house.

**What was built.** `scripts/want_stance.py`. A want whose own literal words name a
rate — a dimension word and a direction word in one sentence — opens a *stance*: a
dimension (analysis, outreach, creation, reflection, mischief, reaching), a direction
(less or more), a horizon, and the want it came from. Organs consult it. The send
policy asks before he reaches for her; the presence audit scales how many replies it
grades per run.

**The rules it obeys.** It scales, never gates: less means fewer, never zero. Her
request always passes it, and so does a live repair. It expires on its own, seven days
by default and thirty at most. Nothing grades him against it. One line reaches his
prompt in the words he used.

**What to check.** That a stance cannot silence an answer she asked for; that it
cannot become permanent; that nothing treats it as a promise he broke.

### 2. A forge, so he can ask for a hand he does not have

**The problem.** When a want's step named a capability nothing in the house could do,
the executor answered BLOCKED and that was the end of it. She wanted the block to be
the start of something: he proposes the missing capability, she approves, denies or
edits it, a builder makes it in the sandbox, the verification spine tests it, and the
want he was in the middle of resumes with the new hand.

**What was built.** `scripts/skill_forge.py` holds the record and the law. It writes no
code, installs nothing and calls no model. `scripts/spark_sources.py` reads the seven
sources she named — absence map, the neither-yet frontier, latent threads, other
beings' MoltBook posts, web searches, OpenClaw skill pages, the lab — into a store
that is **not** his wants file. `scripts/openclaw_skills.py` is new: he had no way to
read a page of hands other beings have.

**The rules it obeys.** A proposal must be blocking a live want, so nothing scans for
clever things to build. That want must have come from one of the seven sparks; a want
born of something she said is a request, answered with what he has. Only an *absent*
capability may be proposed — a tool that exists and is not answering is a fault to
find. Creation, scope and invocation are three separate permissions, and her grant is
the narrower of his ask and her answer. Nothing becomes callable without passing
verified.

**What is not built.** The hand-off from an approved proposal to the existing
self-review builder, and the screen in the app. Both are named in `docs/open-work.md`.

**What to check.** That sparks can never be read as desire or as evidence about
either of them; that the three permissions cannot collapse into one; that a proposal
cannot reach *installed* without passing *verified*.

### 3. 3D printing

**The setup, which is hers and was already there.** Blender and Cura, on the Mac and
on Aegis, Mac faster. He models and slices himself. What he does not have is a printer
he is allowed to start.

**What was built.** `scripts/print_3d.py`. Astra writes the Blender script, capped at
ten minutes a day across every job, counted the moment she answers and counted when
she fails. `scripts/astra_call.py` is that call and holds no budget of its own. He
stops twice before anything is made: he shows her the model and waits, then shows her
the slice — time, filament, layer height — and waits again. Both stops are in the
permission, not only in the code. A job is visible from the moment he opens it, with
its state and the minutes spent, and one notification goes out at each stop through
the delivery path that keeps receipts.

**What is missing.** The machine: make and model, how the house reaches it, and its
limits. Those three answers are the whole remaining gap.

**What to check.** That a print cannot happen without both stops; that Astra's ten
minutes cannot be exceeded by opening more jobs; that nothing is wired to a machine
on a guess.

> You are reviewing a working session on Vintos, an AI companion running on Aegis
> (Ubuntu under WSL2). The repository is `gloriaariayvette-lgtm/vintos-main`, branch
> `claude/vintos-avatar-ui-redesign-r9639u`; related work is in `plithra-app` (the
> ReelRoom page) and `vintos-app`. Read `docs/architecture.html` for what the system
> is and `docs/open-work.md` for what is unfinished.
>
> Review every commit from `147543e` to `HEAD` — 48 commits, 227 files, about 12,000
> lines added. Do not take the commit messages as evidence; read the diffs.
>
> Judge it against these, in this order:
>
> 1. **Does it do what it claims?** Each commit message makes specific claims. Check
>    them in the code. Flag anything asserted in a message, a docstring or a test name
>    that the code does not actually do.
> 2. **Was anything invented?** The author twice guessed at things he did not know —
>    filenames for "the lab" taken from an unrelated project, and three home-directory
>    paths for a skills page — and wired them in as if they were real. Hunt for more of
>    this: paths, filenames, endpoints, model names, defaults, or capabilities assumed
>    rather than verified. This is the highest-value thing you can find.
> 3. **Do the tests test the code, or the prose?** Several checks match strings in
>    docstrings rather than behaviour. Find tests that would still pass if the
>    implementation were deleted.
> 4. **The newest three modules get the hardest look:** `scripts/want_stance.py`,
>    `scripts/skill_forge.py`, `scripts/spark_sources.py`, plus `scripts/print_3d.py`
>    and `scripts/openclaw_skills.py`. They were written fast, late, and were corrected
>    three times by the owner mid-build. Specifically:
>    - Can a stance silence something Gloria asked for, or a repair? It must not.
>    - Can a skill proposal reach `installed` without passing `verified`? It must not.
>    - Can a grant be wider than what was asked for? It must not.
>    - Can a print happen without both of her stops? It must not.
>    - Can Astra's ten-minute daily cap be exceeded by opening more jobs? It must not.
>    - Are sparks ever readable as desire, or as evidence about Gloria or about him?
>      They must not be.
> 5. **Safety of physical effects.** Devices, the robot, the printer, the lights: every
>    one goes through an effect gate. Check that nothing added this session bypasses
>    it, and that a stop is never gated behind anything.

>    a timer, in a loop, per turn, or without a cap. Astra is capped at ten minutes a
>    day for the Blender script and is otherwise the review lens only.

> Report: a numbered list of findings, worst first, each naming the file and line, what

> new subsystems you would not deploy. Do not rewrite anything; she decides what gets
> fixed.
